At Bio-synthetic, the first instrument you encounter may already be growing beside you. Across an afternoon, a night and the following sunrise at Jungli near Dandeli, Fatty Recording Co. invites you to experience interspecies music among plants, electronic instruments and human performers. Bring your curiosity, and allow your sense of concert time to loosen. A phrase may return transformed; an apparently quiet plant may become the centre of attention. You are welcome to follow every performance, return to an installation, or sleep before gathering again at dawn.

The festival explores Expanding Parameters through a question: where does composition begin when its materials come from a living organism? Music & its Others also runs through the gathering, asking how musical attention might extend beyond human performers. Here, interspecies music describes a practice of composing relationships between human decisions and plant-derived variation. Biosonification translates measured electrical variations into sound through a designed system. The synthesizer tones you hear depend on sensors, software and artistic decisions. Each artist brings a different instrument and a different answer to how these contributions can meet.[1]

In adotj's solo, Between Leaf and Circuit, that relationship becomes audible through BECA, the biosynth developed at Fatty Recording Co. Its performer selects scale, root note, octave range and tempo, establishing a musical framework for incoming plant data. Sensitivity and rhythmic settings further shape the unfolding material. Synthesizers and MIDI controllers allow adotj to sculpt its sound during performance. Listen for how a changed register opens space, or how a new pitch collection alters the character of familiar activity. The sequence remains generative within conditions that a musician can deliberately compose.

The afternoon Leaf Library places these live practices beside recorded music. Mort Garson's Mother Earth's Plantasia imagines plants as listeners through melodic Moog compositions. Its carefully arranged tunes offer a historical counterpart to today's live systems.[2] Mamoru Fujieda's Patterns of Plants takes another route: patterns drawn from plant measurements become material for composed instrumental miniatures, heard here in Sarah Cahill's piano recordings.[3] Selections from Plantland Radio extend the comparison, including Tom Iddan's combination of plant signals, percussion and field recordings.[4] These encounters ask you to hear the distance between inspiration, translation and composition.

Modern Biology brings plant-driven electronics into an improvised encounter with an acoustic guest, extending a practice that has included moss, synthesizers and clarinet.[5] Sustained acoustic tones can leave electronic figures exposed, while shorter phrases offer opportunities for reply. At twilight, Wild Anima's commissioned set brings voice and field recordings into the exchange, drawing on her practice of assembling sound collages.[4] Masterplants Orchestra then broadens the scale of listening through several plant inputs.[6] Individual lines will be introduced separately before overlapping, allowing their differing rates of activity to shape an ensemble texture.

Roots in Conversation brings adotj together with invited Manganiyar musicians from Rajasthan. Developed during a residency, the commission gives the musicians responsibility for choosing songs, instrumentation and the terms of collaboration. Unaccompanied passages establish their phrasing before BECA enters. A shared tonal centre and agreed tempo can support exchanges between recurring vocal or instrumental phrases and plant-derived patterns. At other moments, percussion may withdraw, leaving space around the voice. The interest lies in how performers negotiate timing and repetition across distinct musical practices, with room for either partner to leave a phrase unanswered.

Between concerts, Mileece's commissioned Leaf Room offers a small, spatial listening environment informed by her participatory plant installations.[7] Biosis contributes an overnight installation combining plant input, synthesizers and environmental recordings, extending the project's existing approach.[4] One BECA station retains the same mapping from afternoon into morning, inviting return visits. Low-volume speakers and headphones keep these encounters intimate. Brian d'Souza's late-night Plants Can Dance performance opens another electronic possibility, drawing on his use of biodata and modular synthesis.[8] Rhythm may emerge, dissolve or persist; its direction remains a discovery shared with the audience.

Jungli's community setting allows meals, conversation and rest to belong to the programme.[9] Performances occupy existing gathering areas, with musicians and plants at ground level. Small speaker arrays surround listeners without overwhelming the site's own sounds. Accessible routes connect seating, shelters and a quiet resting area; headphones carry the continuing installation through the deepest hours. In the BECA workshop, participants can hear one input through different scales and tempos, discovering how their decisions change the result. Participation also includes simply waiting. A sparse passage deserves the same attention as a dense one.

Before sunrise, Anoushka Shankar joins adotj for First Light, a commissioned interspecies jugalbandi between sitar, BECA and plants. Shankar's work across classical, contemporary and electronic settings provides a starting point for this encounter.[10] The performers establish pitch material and a tonal reference together, then explore melodic response, sustained resonance and silence. A selected scale provides a pitch resource; the sitar's phrasing and ornamentation remain expressive decisions. Keeping BECA's mapping stable for part of the performance invites attention to incoming changes around dawn. Those changes remain open to interpretation: this is a musical observation, with weather, sensors and artistic mediation also present.

The festival's dithered leaves and ASCII fragments carry this process into its visual identity. Leaf shapes become discrete marks, echoing the translation from measurement into musical events. Restrained projections fall on freestanding fabric and gradually fade as daylight makes the actual foliage visible. By then, electronic tones, sitar resonance and the surrounding soundscape may be difficult to separate in memory. Stay for breakfast. The final experience of Bio-synthetic belongs as much to the attention you carry away as to any sound produced during the night.
